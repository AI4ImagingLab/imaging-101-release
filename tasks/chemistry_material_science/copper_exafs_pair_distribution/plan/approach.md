# Implemented first-shell cumulant inversion

The selected method is FEFF-path fitting of a reduced real Cu K-edge spectrum using the first two structural cumulants. The published 2001 method equations (1)–(3) define the scattering-path contribution and complex momentum disorder correction; section 3.2 defines complex R-space nonlinear least squares. The bound example is the modern Larch one-shell fitting example applied to the separately released real 10 K copper spectrum, not the CoPt3 multicontrast demonstration in the 2001 paper or all paths in the upstream tutorial collection. No synthetic observation or raw-count background extraction is performed in this task. The released background-subtracted chi(k) is legitimate reduced observation data, not a target-derived structural answer.

Unknown vector p=(A,DeltaE,sigma2,DeltaR) has units (1,eV,Angstrom²,Angstrom). R0=2.5478 Angstrom and coordination N=12 define the supplied nominal elemental Cu path. They are prior model geometry; the fitted mean is R0+DeltaR, not a hidden target constant. The public tables have 59 samples: total phase is original columns `real[2*phc]+phase[feff]`, effective amplitude is `mag[feff]*red_factor`, and complex momentum is `real[p]+i/lambda`. Its units are 1/Angstrom, amplitude is Angstrom and mean free path is Angstrom. There is no target-estimated calibration. Interpolation is SciPy cubic UnivariateSpline with s=0 and default polynomial extrapolation, matching the source. Replacing it with linear interpolation can change phase-sensitive fitting.

Resample observations onto k_j=0.05j, j=0,...,499 by linear interpolation of the original stored k/chi coordinates. Do not overwrite the raw k values: eight source coordinates differ from the nominal uniform axis by approximately 10^-6 Angstrom^-1. All 500 measured points remain bundled. This subtlety was verified numerically and the parity test explicitly performs upstream interpolation before transforming the data.

Use ETOK = 1/[10^20 hbar²/(2 m_e e)] from SciPy SI constants. For each nominal k form energy variable u=k²-DeltaE*ETOK, then q=sign(u)*sqrt(abs(u)). If any |u|<10^-6, replace entries |u|<1.5e-6 by +1e-6, exactly as the source. This low-k convention avoids the singularity and lies outside the useful tapered range. Interpolate phase delta, amplitude F, real momentum and mean free path at q. Set pp=(real[p]+i/lambda)² and p_complex=sqrt(pp), with the principal branch. The local forward expression is

```
cchi = N*A*F/[q*(R0+DeltaR)^2]
       * exp(-2*R0*Im(p_complex)-2*pp*sigma2
             + i*(2*q*R0+delta+2*p_complex*(DeltaR-2*sigma2/R0)))
chi_model = Im(cchi)
```

The first complex sample is replaced by `2*cchi[1]-cchi[2]` before taking its imaginary component. This preserves the published complex-p cumulant correction rather than a simplified real-k damped sinusoid. Third and fourth cumulants and extra imaginary energy are zero; no unused general path framework is vendored. This directly evaluates the closed forward equation. All computation and outputs are float64/complex128.

The taper is the source's improved Kaiser window, not NumPy's generic Kaiser sequence. On k_j=0.05j with 2048 total points, support endpoints are 1 and 19 Angstrom^-1, center 10 and halfwidth9, beta4. Set u=max(0,1-((k-10)/9)²), w=(I0(4 sqrt(u))-1)/(I0(4)-1), where I0 is modified Bessel function order zero. These endpoints come from upstream kmin3/kmax17/dk4 and its index rounding. Zero-pad `chi*w*k²` to 2048 complex samples and apply the negative-sign unnormalized NumPy FFT. Multiply by 0.05/sqrt(pi), retain first1024 samples. The radius spacing is pi/(2048*0.05) Angstrom, reflecting the 2kR phase. Index bounds `int(.01+1.4/Rstep)` inclusive and `int(.01+3/Rstep)` exclusive select52 complex values; residual is104 interleaved real/imaginary values.

No measured pointwise noise is available. Explicitly use equal input epsilon_k=1 as a harmless global fit normalization, not a fabricated experimental uncertainty. Source epsilon_R = 1/[2 sqrt(5pi/(0.05*(17^5-3^5)))] = 33.61090488089081. Divide complex residual by epsilon_R, then interleave real and imaginary parts. The optimizer minimizes the unnormalized residual squared sum, with no mean division, no regularizer and no background refinement. The source later reports chi squared with the independent-information factor N_idp/N_residual; this reporting convention is not silently relabeled as the optimization objective. N_idp=1+2*(3-1.4)*(17-3)/pi. R factor is complex residual squared norm divided by data squared norm on the same52 Fourier radii, independent of the arbitrary scalar epsilon normalization.

Initial p=[1,0,0,0] preserves the one-shell example. The variance starts at zero because it is a forward-model parameter and no Gaussian radial output is requested until after fitting. A separate oracle rerun starts [.8,2,.003,.005]. Use scipy.optimize.leastsq (MINPACK LM), no bounds, ftol=xtol=gtol=1e-6, epsfcn=1e-10, maxfev10000. The selected step-size/trust-region and numerical derivatives are the general MINPACK defaults, not a custom inverse update. The maxfev is a finite evaluation safety bound; actual convergence takes approximately31 residual evaluations. There is no task-specific line search or invented iteration formula. Accept only MINPACK flags1–4, finite parameters and positive final pair variance; otherwise fail clearly. No physical clipping is used to change the solution.

After inversion, construct the Gaussian first-shell density g(r)=12 exp(-(r-R0-DeltaR)²/(2 sigma2))/sqrt(2pi sigma2) on r=1.8+0.002j, j=0,...,800. It has units neighbors/Angstrom and integral12 over the unbounded physical radial approximation. This is the explicit Gaussian interpretation of two cumulants and fixed coordination, not a measured unique distribution or independent ground truth. Structural disorder and thermal variance are not separately identified by a single low-temperature spectrum. Report parameters in addition to the profile to prevent a visually similar density from hiding inconsistent amplitude/energy fits.

Evaluation compares both quantities with independently captured standard inversion outputs and also checks the measured first-shell forward R factor.
