# Implemented spherical SESANS correlation inversion

The task reuses all40 actual polystyrene SESANS reduced measurements released in sasmodels/example/spheres2micron.ses (2014-07-10). It implements the sphere forward/correlation model from the repository's sesans_sphere_2micron example with a conventional linear-SESANS Hankel projection, then the original Bumps scipy.leastsq fitting option. Nominal two-micrometer particle diameter is descriptive sample information, not an exact truth. The method is not changed to radial-geometry SESANS; Kadletz2022 section1.2 explicitly documents the same conventional linear-SESANS physics, which was available in full and independently checked. The2008 analysis paper was bibliographically identified and previewed but its complete text was inaccessible; it is not represented as fully read.

Unknown p=(R,phi), in units (Angstrom,dimensionless), parameterizes an isotropic monodisperse homogeneous sphere with no structure factor. The variance amplitude is phi(1-phi), as in the upstream expression. Known particle/solvent SLDs1.41/2.70 are numerical multiples of10^-6 Angstrom^-2. Let V=4piR³/3 and f(x)=3(sin x-x cos x)/x³. Use the exact original float64 small-x branch: for |x|<0.1, evaluate `1+x²*(-3/30+x²*(3/840+x²*(-3/45360)))`; otherwise evaluate `3*(sin(x)/x-cos(x))/x²`. This four-term expansion preserves the C source rather than naively subtracting near-equal sine/cosine terms.

The macroscopic differential cross section is `I(q)=phi*(1-phi)*[1e-4*(contrast*V*f(qR))²]/V`, with contrast=1.41-2.70 in the stored SLD units. The1e-4 factor combines10^-12 from squared stored SLD with10^8 converting inverse Angstrom to inverse centimeter. I is cm^-1. Radius/diameter and units are not interchanged. The source's form-factor kernel explicitly normalizes by V; no domain solver remains in runtime.

For the supplied40 lengths z, qmin=0.1*2pi/(40*z[-1]), qmax=2pi/(z[1]-z[0]). Generate q=exp(arange(log(qmin),log(qmax),log(1.0003))) with the upper endpoint excluded, giving32216 samples. Backward differences dq=q[i]-q[i-1] are used, with dq[0] set to dq[1]. The scalar integration row H0_i=dq_i*q_i/(2pi). Set H_ij=J0(q_i*z_j)*dq_i*q_i/(2pi). The explicit output is `D=H.T@I-H0@I`, not exp(D). D is the published reduced logarithmic depolarisation in Angstrom^-2 cm^-1. Its relationship to directly measured polarization is P/P0=exp(t*lambda²*D), t=.2cm, wavelength2.11Angstrom. Source observation values and their uncertainty arrays remain exact; reported length errors are retained but no extra error-in-variable or wavelength smearing is introduced.

Acceptance investigation: upstream direct_model._make_sesans_transform converts theta_max=.0168radian into q_acceptance=(2pi/max(lambda))*sin(theta_max), then SesansTransform._set_hankel compares **arcsin(q*lambda/(2pi))**, an angle, against that q-valued number. This is dimensionally inconsistent. An independent physical angle calculation shows max sampled angle about.00180radian, below the actual.0168radian limit as well as the source's numeric q-valued limit. Thus no quadrature node is masked under either interpretation. The cleaned implementation uses the physically dimensioned angle limit, explicitly correcting this conditional's units, and frozen H-row, H0, whole-grid angle and forward parity tests prove that the correction changes **no value on the bound observations**. This is not an unqualified claim of unchanged behavior for other datasets: narrower apertures or other quadrature grids must be reevaluated. No acceptance information is inferred from the fitted target. All source discrepancy evidence is retained in validation.md.

The residual sign is `(D_model-D_measured)/standard_error`. Objective is0.5 times its squared sum, without division by40 or any regularizer. The original Bumps LevenbergMarquardtFit also appends13 zero residuals for the uniform/fixed parameter priors in this selected sphere experiment. These contribute no information within bounds but are preserved in the bound-penalty vector length. Radius bounds[100,100000]Angstrom and fraction bounds[.001,.5] preserve the example and choose the particle phase below one half. For a proposed p, compute clipped p and stray=clipped-p. Evaluate the40 measurement residuals at clipped p and append13 zeros. If sum(stray²)>0, add1e6 to that stray sum; add `sign(residual)*stray_cost/53` to all53 components. This explicitly preserves the source's projected-bound penalty, rather than substituting a different constrained optimizer. Final p is clipped as by the original driver, after a successful MINPACK termination.

Run scipy.optimize.leastsq with initialization[10000,.0855], ftol=xtol=1.5e-8, epsfcn=1e-8 and maxfev=600 (200 source steps times3 for two variables). Other MINPACK controls, including gtol0 and factor100, are defaults of the source's selected SciPy fitter. There is no custom step size, line search, proximal update or hidden initialization. Its trust-region/damping and numerical derivatives are general optimization primitives. Only convergence flags1–4 and finite parameters count as successful in the local solver; Bumps' high-level generic success field is not blindly trusted. The independent oracle used the unchanged original FitDriver/LevenbergMarquardtFit directly, avoiding unrelated web UI imports while retaining its complete inverse update/bound behavior.

Reconstruction is the dimensionless single-sphere two-phase indicator autocovariance `C(r)=phi*(1-phi)*(1-3u/4+u³/16)` for u=r/R<=2, zero above2. The radial grid is r=100jAngstrom, j=0,...,500. Its zero-separation amplitude is phi(1-phi), normalized overlap at r=R is5/16, and support ends at2R. It is a bulk model correlation, not a recovered particle image, a measured true density or a general interacting-particle correlation. The finite radius/fraction inference and projection do not uniquely establish more complex microstructures.

Independent physical checks use the zero-q sphere volume limit, Guinier coefficient1-(qR)²/5 for intensity, nonnegative/finite-support sphere overlap covariance, phase-exchange symmetry phi versus1-phi, and the analytic total projected scattering integral `G0=1.5 R*contrast²*1e-4*phi*(1-phi)`. Finite q integration/log discretization differs from the infinite integral by approximately0.048percent; rtol.002 was fixed in the preimplementation physics test, accounting for both finite-q truncation and log rectangle discretization without relaxing upstream parity tolerances. The reference solver uses the finite discretization, not this infinite-integral limit.

Evaluation uses an independent standard inversion reference rather than truth and scores autocovariance NRMSE, normalized parameter error, and measured depolarisation chi squared. Independent upstream reruns from[10000,.0855] and[12000,.07] differ by profile NRMSE9.937924697021348e-8 and maximum parameter error4.291900515454472e-7 (normalizers10000Angstrom and.1). Predeclared limits are2e-5,1e-5,and40.4 respectively; parameter agreement tolerance is about23times the measured independent optimizer variation, and the observation boundary is about1percent above the standard method's39.98566457. The observed reduced chi squared is1.0523 for38 measurement degrees of freedom, compatible with the stated noise approximation but not independent proof of microscopic correctness.
