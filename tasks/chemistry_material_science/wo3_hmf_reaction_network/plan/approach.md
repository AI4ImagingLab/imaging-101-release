# Implemented reaction inversion

The complete network and symbol definitions are given self-contained in README. State c=(u,v,w) contains fifteen molecular concentrations in micromol/L; time t is hours; rates (a,b,d,e,f,g,h,i,j,k,l) are h^-1. All five degradation transfers and five final transfers conserve total modeled molecular concentration; this is a compartment-model law rather than independently established molecular stoichiometry for unknown products. Corrected electrical weights are n=(0,2,2,4,6,2,4,4,6,8,4,6,6,8,10), ordered by state. Charge is F V sum(n [c(t)-c(0)]*1e-6), F=96485.33212331001 C/mol, V=0.1 L. Known oxidation arrows each transfer two electrons; each unknown degradation layer is assigned an additional two electrons only as a phenomenological assumption. The source index-based weights and omission of initial-charge subtraction are confirmed errors, explicitly corrected before the independent corrected-source reruns and current local implementation.

Use the original pandas CSV parser values exported as float64 observations. For each same-index row and column of the three repeats, average with Kahan compensated accumulation, matching pandas groupby.mean. Mean initial chromatographic concentrations initialize u; v=w=0. Electrical time and chemical time are separately averaged. All 30 assay rows (through 65h) and 3000 electrical rows (through 64h) are fitted; the output prediction grid is 150 points over 0–64h. There is no spatial grid or boundary flux; the model is a well-mixed closed reaction compartment. No synthetic data is generated.

Integrate the explicit mass-action derivatives with scipy.integrate.odeint, retaining its original default tolerances and LSODA method selection. No Jacobian, custom step size, objective gradient or adjoint is required by the selected method. Rates remain constant during each integration. The objective is sum of squares of r=(y-z)/(y+z), with r=0 where y=z=0. Concatenate chemical residuals species first, time second, then charge residuals. Do not divide by sample count or experimental variance. The high electrical sampling density therefore contributes its full original weight. Reported experimental replicate variability is descriptive rather than used to reweight the fit.

Minimize with MINPACK Levenberg–Marquardt through scipy.optimize.leastsq. The original lmfit lower-bound transform is k=sqrt(z^2+1)-1 and its initial inverse is z=sqrt((k+1)^2-1). Run six independent uniform physical initial rates0.005,0.01,0.025,0.05,0.1,0.2h^-1, each with the same original LM settings, and select the minimum residual sum of squares. This is a disclosed reliability extension required after correcting the source charge formula; a single0.05 start reaches a poor local optimum. Parameters are updated by MINPACK's damped least-squares rule on the unconstrained z vector. Defaults explicitly preserved: ftol=xtol=1.5e-8, gtol=0, epsfcn=1e-10, factor=100, no analytic Jacobian or scaling diagonal. The evaluation budget is 24000, well above each actual independently captured fit. Termination uses MINPACK relative objective/step tests, not a custom rate criterion or line search. No regularizer is added.

Floating-point order matters: convert each concentration to mol/L before multiplying each integer electron weight, stack fifteen weighted columns and sum axis zero, then multiply F*V. Matrix multiplication followed by scaling changes ulps and near-zero boundary convergence. Exact source parser, compensated mean and charge operation order recover every original rate and all full-fit outputs on the validated backend. This fidelity is not evidence that the weak near-zero HMFCA degradation rate is physically determined.

Evaluate every rate separately by absolute error <=2e-5h^-1+0.002*abs(reference rate). Evaluate each of all fifteen state trajectories and charge by dynamic-range NRMSE <=0.002, with no crop, magnitude, alignment or rescaling. These are method agreement thresholds. An early optimization with reordered charge summation failed the pre-frozen hidden-pool criteria, and was corrected without changing thresholds. Original profiles and initialization perturbations show kD22 weakly identified; keep that warning in results. Use analytic conservation, positivity and balanced half-reaction checks for all five known arrows, both complete pathways, and Q(t0)=0 checks independently of numerical parity.
