# Implemented mathematics and numerical conventions

The runtime reconstructs the bound sixteen by 89 observations, on the 4584-cell Roche surface. All geometry, disk integration, local line shape, derivatives and inverse updates are executable local mathematics. Data are real already normalized LSD profiles; no true surface map is available. The benchmark reference is the reproduced standard method.

## Acquisition and preprocessing

`load_data` removes only the leading batch axis. No interpolation, renormalization, smoothing or covariance correction is performed. Original selection of inclusive velocities Vr+[-80,80] occurred before bundling. Wavelength is 650[1+(v+19.8)/299792.458] nm. Intensity errors are unchanged (source chiScaleI=1). Diagonal errors assume independent weighting although an LSD covariance matrix is unavailable.

The midpoint mesh and Roche model are defined in README. Surface radius is normalized to the polar radius. A cell area is delta_phi times the integral r(theta)^2 sin(theta)/cos(gamma) dtheta, where gamma is the angle between the radial direction and the equipotential normal. Adaptive general-purpose quadrature has the original defaults. In normalized units the effective gravity components are gr=-1/r^2+(8/27)r w^2 sin(theta)^2 and gt=(8/27)r w^2 sin(theta)cos(theta). Their norm to power 0.5 gives the gravity-darkening factor; the normal is the opposite potential gradient, pointing outward. Views use longitude -Omega(theta)(JD-JDref), inclination pi/4. The Cartesian rotational velocity is (-r sin(theta)sin(phi),r sin(theta)cos(phi),0) divided by the radius of the nearest-equatorial mesh cell, scaled by vsini/sin(inclination), then by Omega(theta)/Omega_equator. The projected recession velocity is minus its dot product with the viewing vector.

The source parameter name radiusEq is misleading: its Roche formula uses the supplied .72 solar radii as the polar normalization. This formula, rather than the name, is preserved. The period/vsini-implied physical radius differs from .72; they are separately adopted velocity and shape parameters, not forced into a new model. Req/Rp is about 1.022631 and w about .377942. No physical radius estimate is reconstructed.

Instrumental Gaussian width is (c/65000)*.6005612043932249 km/s. The total Gaussian width aG is sqrt(2.41^2+instrument_width^2); the Lorentz ratio becomes .89*2.41/aG and local depth becomes calibrated_depth*2.41/aG, preserving the Lorentz HWHM and line area. Local wavelength is observed rest wavelength divided by (1+projected_velocity/c). The explicit four-region Humlicek rational approximation evaluates the complex Voigt function with the original coefficients; it is not replaced by another profile. The absorption is L=1-depth*Re(H). Its wavelength derivative is depth/delta_lambda*2[-u Re(H)+a Im(H)], with u=(lambda0-lambda)/delta_lambda and a the adjusted Lorentz ratio. This derivative and the signed weak-field factor in README give D.

The intrinsic line depth starts at .6306 only for equivalent-width calibration. Mean measured width is the right-rectangle sum over all intensity samples, including a last-bin repeat. At uniform brightness and zero field, fit depth by SciPy's scalar Brent minimizer of the absolute mean-width difference, bracket (.006306,.6306,63.06), tolerance 1e-5. This is nuisance calibration from visible measurements, not a fitted map supplied to the solver. Both tasks obtain about .647774. The full numeric value is tested against the original capture.

## Spatial unknown and objective

Let d be the selected measured channel flattened epoch-first, sigma its supplied standard deviation, F the local forward calculation, and R=dF/dimage. The chi-square is C=sum[(F-d)^2/sigma^2], without division by sample count; the reduced diagnostic here is C/1424 (not a degrees-of-freedom estimate). Its gradient is 2 R^T[(F-d)/sigma^2].

For positive brightness b, entropy S=sum_j -w_j[b_j(log(b_j/m)-1)+m], m=1 and w=cell area. Gradient is w(log(m)-log(b)), second derivative -w/b, inverse negative curvature f=b/w. All cells start at one. The brightness derivative of a normalized profile is W_j/sum(W b)*(L_j-I), including continuum normalization.

For magnetic coefficients a, define psi=sqrt(a^2+4mB^2), mB=100 G. Signed entropy is sum_j w_j[psi-2mB-a log((psi+a)/(2mB))], gradient -w log((psi+a)/(2mB)), curvature -w/psi and f=psi/w. Weights equal spherical-harmonic degree l, repeated for six real/imaginary coefficient blocks. The full vector expansion has 135 (l,m) pairs, l=1,...,15 and m=0,...,l, and 810 packed real numbers: alpha real/imaginary, beta real/imaginary, gamma real/imaginary. All begin at zero. No l=0 magnetic monopole is included.

With Condon–Shortley associated Legendre P, c_lm=sqrt[(2l+1)/(4pi)*(l-m)!/(l+m)!], Y=c_lm P exp(i m phi), X=i m c_lm P exp(i m phi)/[(l+1)sin(theta)], Z=c_lm (dP/dtheta)exp(i m phi)/(l+1). The physical outputs are Br=Re(alpha Y), Btheta=-Re(beta Z+gamma X), Bphi=-Re(beta X-gamma Z). Their Cartesian transformation uses the usual outward radial, southward colatitude and increasing-longitude orthonormal basis. The derivative arrays carry complex conjugates to match the packed real/imaginary convention. In shared SciPy 1.17, lpmv and the explicit derivative recurrence (x^2-1)Pprime=l*x*P-(l+m)Pprevious replace removed lpmn. All midpoint cells avoid poles. Frozen original basis and finite differences validate the replacement; original oracle uses SciPy 1.15.3 unchanged.

## Inverse updates and stopping

The selected standard method maximizes entropy while fitting C to 1424. Each iteration constructs at most ten Euclidean-normalized directions starting with f*grad(C), f*grad(S), then repeated 2f R^T diag(1/sigma^2)R on earlier directions. The entropy metric is diagonal -Ssecond. Its projected eigensystem removes eigenvalues at or below 1e-8 times the largest absolute eigenvalue and whitens surviving directions. A second eigendecomposition diagonalizes the projected likelihood Hessian. In this subspace E, define Cmu=E^T grad(C), Smu=E^T grad(S), Hessian eigenvalues gamma, and trust length L0^2=.3*Itot. For brightness Itot=sum(w*b); for magnetism Itot=sum(w*max(abs(a),mB)).

The quadratic unconstrained C minimum is Cmin=C-.5 sum(Cmu^2/gamma). The step target is max(1424,.66666667*Cmin+.33333333*C). The control loop brackets entropy multiplier alpha and penalty P, beginning alphaMin=max(0,-min(gamma)); trial subspace coefficients are x=(alpha*Smu-Cmu)/(P+gamma+alpha). It computes Cq=C+Cmu dot x+.5 sum(gamma*x^2), Cqp with gamma+P, and length sum(x^2). Nested chopping changes alpha and P until the target/length conditions hold (relative tolerance 1e-5, relaxed target 1.001). These explicit branches are retained locally in `control`, `chopUp`, `chopDown`; no generic optimizer replaces the published update. Apply image += E*x, with a 1e-6 brightness floor and no magnetic clipping.

After each evaluated image, the code records its S,C and Test=.5*norm(normalized gradS-normalized gradC)^2 (zero-gradient normalization is zero), then proposes the next image. Stop when C<=1424*1.001 and Test<1e-4, or after 20 iterations. This reproduces the source's proposal/output convention: brightness packing aliases the map and therefore its final proposal is applied in place; magnetic packing copies coefficients and the final proposal is not unpacked once convergence is detected. Final stored original spectra describe the evaluated image, so independently reproject the returned spatial map for the Judge and fresh notebook residual. The difference is tiny for this converged brightness case and is measured, not hidden. No target-entropy or simultaneous inversion is selected.

## Evaluation and limitations

Use the standard cosine NCC and dynamic-range NRMSE, separately on each required component. Reference is the independent unchanged source run, not truth. A nonzero tolerance 1e-7 in dynamic-range NRMSE and NCC>=.999999999 covers floating-point eigensystem/Legendre differences while rejecting uniform, zero, permuted, scaled and wrong-sign maps; measurement chi-square/sample must also be <=1.002. There is no alignment or amplitude normalization. The hidden observation checker sums independently captured original, target-independent local profiles and geometric continuum weights against the submitted spatial output. It never imports the solver. Both reference agreement and measurement fit are method reproducibility tests, not evidence of unique physical truth. Weak-field, single effective-line, fixed-atmosphere and adopted geometry assumptions are preserved.
