# Implemented mathematics

The physical unknown is a pair of apparent fluorescence lifetime maps, not a unique multicomponent molecular decomposition. The selected implementation combines the published phasor representation with standard phase/modulation apparent-lifetime definitions as implemented in PhasorPy v0.7. Digman et al. (2008), page L16, explicitly do not resolve lifetime components; this task does not claim that their paper supplied the apparent-lifetime extension or that its original FRET experiments are reproduced. The entire native 256×256 detector field and its 256 delay bins are retained. Counts are uint16 exactly as decoded by upstream. The separate fluorescein image is pooled with uint64 accumulation; these are acquisition-independent standard photons, not sample-derived calibration.

For pixel p and bin k, h[p,k] is the photon count, N=256, θ[k]=2πk/N and T=24.99989318828099 ns. Time is t[k]=kT/N, not bin centers. Sample and calibration histories obey a periodic convolution with instrument response. The positive-sine convention is z=(Σh cos θ+iΣh sin θ)/Σh; the normalized mean returned by normalized_phasor is Σh/N. Zero total yields undefined NaN phasors internally, later excluded by support.

The known calibration lifetime tc=4.1 ns has zt=(1+iωtc)/(1+(ωtc)²), ω=2π·40.00017089948058·10⁻³ ns⁻¹. The intensity-weighted phasor of all calibration photons is zc. The instrument transfer is zc/zt. Thus Z=z·zt/zc=G+iS. Calibration multiplication is a rotation and dilation, matching upstream phasor_center(mean,...) then phasor_calibrate(mean method). Pooling before DFT is algebraically identical to intensity-weighted averaging.

Phase lifetime τφ=S/(ωG) and modulation lifetime τm=sqrt(1/(G²+S²)−1)/ω are reported in ns. There is no inverse iteration, loss normalization, regularization, initialization, step size or stopping criterion: this is a closed-form harmonic inverse. No single exponential is fitted to the complete sample histogram. The difference between the two apparent lifetimes is retained because it contains scientifically useful mixture information.

The fixed mask requires total measured photons ≥200, G>0, S>0, and G²+S²<1. It uses the measured sample plus independently known calibration only. Both maps are zero outside this support. No negative/complex lifetime is silently clipped inside the mask. This quality rule was fixed at upstream capture before evaluating the cleaned implementation. No histogram shift, median filter, denoising or spatial interpolation is performed.

Evaluation compares complete batch-first maps to the separately reproduced upstream standard-method result. It is reference agreement, not physical truth. NCC is flattened uncentered cosine similarity; NRMSE is RMS error divided by reference maximum minus minimum. Required thresholds are NCC≥0.999999 and NRMSE≤10⁻⁶ for each map. A nonzero float64 tolerance is justified by independently executed upstream Cython and local NumPy pathways plus analytic exponential and calibration tests, rather than an exact self-comparison. The full decay cannot be fit-consistency tested as a single exponential because the sample is multicomponent. Instead the Judge independently reconstructs dimensionless first-harmonic coordinates from the two submitted apparent lifetimes: radius R=1/sqrt(1+(ωτm)²), G=R/sqrt(1+(ωτφ)²), S=ωτφG. The RMS mismatch of concatenated G,S coordinates against the measured calibrated first harmonic on valid support must be at most 10⁻⁶, without a dynamic-range normalization; its exact definition is recorded separately from image NRMSE.
