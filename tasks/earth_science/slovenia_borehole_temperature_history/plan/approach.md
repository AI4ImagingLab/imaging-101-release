# Implemented inverse mathematics

For each of seven native measured profiles, depth z is positive downward in metres and temperature T is in degrees Celsius. The 21 unknown anomalies x are constant over successive 30-year intervals before the measurement year, in newest-to-oldest order. Diffusivity kappa=1e-6 m²/s and one year=31557600 s. The half-space heat-response matrix is the difference between complementary error functions at consecutive elapsed-time boundaries. The zero-time response at positive depth is zero; the surface row is [1,0,...,0]. A boundary anomaly that remains constant across all bins telescopes to a single response at630 years. No advection, heterogeneous conductivity or transient internal heat source is modeled.

The implemented erfc is the original polynomial approximation: t=1/(1+|u|/2), followed by t exp(−u²−1.26551223+t P(t)). P has coefficients1.00002368,0.37409196,0.09678418,−0.18628806,0.27886807,−1.13520398,1.48851587,−0.82215223,0.17087277 in ascending powers. For negative arguments use2−erfc(|u|). Retaining this approximation avoids replacing the author's discretized operator. Independent comparison with SciPy's analytic special function bounds matrix error on the measured depth domain by6e-8.

Deep measurements with200<=z<=300 fit T=a+bz through ordinary least squares. The design matrix X has columns1 and z. The original scaled-pivot LU inverse of XᵀX and sequential matrix-product accumulation are retained because the covariance calculation subtracts nearly equal quantities. Coefficients c=(XᵀX)^{-1}XᵀT determine the central anomaly y=T−a−bz. No target-derived background is distributed publicly.

For optional author perturbation bands, residual variance is Tᵀ(I−X(XᵀX)^{-1}Xᵀ)T/(n−2), with absolute value when roundoff produces a negative numerator, matching the original almost-perfect-fit branch. Half-widths are the coefficient standard errors multiplied by the original Student-t grid quantile: the first1e-5 grid point whose cumulative probability reaches p−1e-5, where p=(1+float32(.95))/2. The original anomaly routine then doubles these widths. Its positive-slope perturbation is d(z)=−2 width(a)+2 width(b)z; negative slopes reverse the intercept term. The three columns are y+d,y,y−d. These are author perturbation bounds, not claimed95% intervals.

The central selected inverse minimizes ||Hx−y||² over the subspace spanned by the two leading right singular vectors of H. With H=USVᵀ, x=V[:,0:2] diag(1/s[0:2]) U[:,0:2]ᵀy. No iterative optimizer, initialization, step size or stopping condition applies to this closed-form inverse. There is no additional objective normalization, positivity projection or temporal shift. All21 bin values and all seven sites are retained. Rank and diffusivity variants in the notebook are labeled sensitivity experiments; they do not redefine the benchmark target.

The standard Judge computes global cosine NCC and global dynamic-range-normalized RMS error. It separately predicts all237 observations using an independently evaluated analytic erfc response and independently refitted deep geothermal trend, then requires the maximum site RMS to be<=.095K. Agreement boundaries NCC>=.999999 and NRMSE<=1e-6 were frozen from the independent original runs before extraction. No physical historical truth exists.

The notebook retains original SVD/PPI/BTI comparison results. The original PPI and BTI routines repeat x[k] in both terms of their intended percentile interpolation; their reported curves are therefore explicitly labeled as author order-statistic outputs with a demonstrated defect. They are not represented as validated corrected uncertainty estimates. The selected central SVD method does not use these routines.
