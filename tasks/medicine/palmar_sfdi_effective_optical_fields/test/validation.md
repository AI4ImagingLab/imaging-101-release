## Important diffusion-domain limitation

The published paper explicitly restricts diffusion to scattering-dominated tissue and sufficiently low spatial frequencies relative to transport length. Independent checks must not incorrectly assert global absorption monotonicity: at high f, the model has R*(2*pi*f)^2 by 3A*muSPrime*(muA+muSPrime), which can INCREASE with absorption. Example muSPrime1,muA.02 -> .04permm gives DC .51718797 -> .41340939 but f.4 response .04430906 -> .04444351. This is a mathematical property of the selected approximation, not a local-port discrepancy; DC absorption monotonicity and the high-frequency asymptote are separately tested. No transport-theory accuracy is claimed in that regime.

The correct primary criterion is paperEq12 scattering dominance muSPrime>>muA and Eq14 fx<<muTransport. The following paragraph estimates maximum fx around0.25to0.33muTransport; Fig4(a) also illustrates approximately12% diffusion error for fx<=.5muTransport under its specific high-albedo simulation. The discussion reports errors within16% at ratio30 below.5muTransport; calibration with independently known similar-albedo phantom can reduce errors, but this dataset supplies only Rcal, not the phantom albedo needed to assert that condition. The continuous h indicator includes2*pi and h>=1 MUST NOT be equated with the paper's frequency threshold. The first h-based interpretation was corrected before task acceptance.
