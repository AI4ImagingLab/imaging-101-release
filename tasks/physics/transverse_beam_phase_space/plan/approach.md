# Implemented mathematics and numerical conventions

The unknown is a complete four-dimensional centered probability distribution. Physical coordinate columns are X(mm),X′(mrad),Y(mm),Y′(mrad). Eight independent 4 x 4 optics matrices transport that vector to four stations under two configurations; each has x,y,u=(x−y)/sqrt 2 profiles, for 24 total. The public analysis protocol and virtual-bin boundaries are defined in README. Native raw signal, wire positions, means and RMS widths are preserved as float64; only format padding is zero, with an explicit mask. The supplied mean and sigma are original PTA raw-statistical observations, not Gaussian-fit columns or hidden reconstruction-derived calibration.

Preprocessing centers native positions using the observed mean and reflects/reverses the vertical direction. A negative signal minimum is shifted to zero, then values below 0.01 times the original unshifted maximum are zeroed. A sample following a jump greater than 0.25 times the thresholded maximum is removed. Linear interpolation to 100 centers−85..85 mm, zero extrapolation, reflecting Gaussian smoothing sigma 1/truncate 4, subtraction of the filtered minimum, and normalization by sum times delta produce observed profiles. This processing does not replace the separately reported RMS widths with a different moment.

For covariance inference, square the three observed widths per group. The cross-plane position moment is (sigma_x²+sigma_y²)/2−sigma_u². Under the supplied uncoupled transport, three horizontal, three vertical and four cross-plane symmetric-covariance coefficients follow three ordinary linear least-squares systems. Their entries are assembled into the full symmetric 4 x 4 covariance Sigma. The same inversion is defined self-contained in README; the Judge independently builds a general 10-column symmetric-matrix design.

The selected corrected source constructs a canonical normal-mode basis. Let J be block diagonal with two blocks[[0,1],[-1,0]]. Compute complex eigenvectors of Sigma J. For each conjugate pair, choose the first eigenvector with negative imaginary symplectic self-product, and scale the pair by sqrt(2/abs(imag(v*Jv))). Form columns of V from real(v),real(i v). Set the initial normalizing matrix to V^-1. Its transformed covariance is diagonal with an equal emittance in each conjugate pair. Crucially, scale each actual pair by its own transformed variance, rather than assigning an independently sorted emittance to an unrelated eigenmode. This explicit V2 correction is inside the original upstream normal-mode construction; it is not retrospective normalization of a recovered result. The final N Sigma N^T=I and x=N^-1 z. Original uncorrected V1 results are retained as a diagnosed comparison.

The sampler uses 35 cells per normalized axis on[−4,4], width h=8/35. Grid centers are −4+(i+1/2)h. The unnormalized Gaussian prior at each center is exp(−||z||²/2)/(2 pi)². Initialize 24 Lagrange functions h_kd(s) to one where the measured profile is positive and zero elsewhere. Functions are represented on 100 measurement centers with linear interpolation and zero extrapolation. At a normalized grid point the unnormalized model weight is the Gaussian prior times the product of all 24 interpolated functions evaluated at d T_k N^-1 z. The complete four-dimensional product is retained, including cross-plane coupling.

One projection update evaluates all 35^4 weights, normalizes their sum, and chooses 1,000,000 voxels with replacement. It then draws each coordinate uniformly inside its selected voxel; noise=0 removes only additional optional jitter and does not remove this within-cell uniform draw. The centered physical particles are transported through the known optics and histogrammed. The selected source histogram edges are centers−delta/2 followed by a final edge last_center+delta, so the final bin is 1.5 delta wide. Histogram densities are normalized again by their sum times delta. This unusual finite convention is preserved explicitly, rather than silently replacing it by point projection.

For observed g and predicted p, the function update is h←h[1+0.2(g/p−1)] where both g and p are positive. At all other positions use ratio 1. The model remains nonnegative, and zero support is preserved. Process x,y,u within each of eight groups in Gauss–Seidel order and repeat five complete sweeps; no early stopping is used. Default local reproducibility seed is 0, which is a maintainer execution setting and not part of the scientific target. The earlier common-random-number comparison is retained only as a private historical diagnostic and is not an automatic acceptance gate. Current deterministic tests freeze actual upstream particle coordinates as operator inputs; a separate unseeded test checks voxel probabilities and within-cell means, variances and boundaries. The complete five-sweep standard run is tested only against the public scientific properties. The public Judge never requires a seed or random trajectory.

Local optimization caches transported coordinates of the fixed 35^4 center grid. This removes repeated matrix products while leaving the interpolated density and sampled update unchanged. It uses below 1 GiB and a single numerical CPU thread. The return history contains all six states including initialization. The output is normalized voxel probability mass and the freshly inferred coordinate matrix, not a saved upstream fit.

Independent evaluation integrates the piecewise-constant voxel distribution. A linear image of one uniform four-dimensional cell is the convolution of up to four uniform intervals, whose widths are h times the absolute projection coefficients. The Judge deposits center masses linearly on a fine one-dimensional grid and convolves the exactly integrated uniform-interval bins. It sums the finite detector bins, applies actual terminal width, and normalizes by the declared equal-center quadrature.32 subdivisions per detector bin give less than 1.51e-5 relative profile difference from 16 subdivisions and 3.15e-6 from 64; the frozen5e-5 numerical allowance is separate from the independently observed 1 million-particle Monte Carlo variation.

Every individual profile is bounded, together with nonnegative unit mass, whitening consistency and discrete KL relative to the Gaussian midpoint prior. KL uses normalized finite-cell probabilities, not an incorrectly labeled exact continuum entropy. There is no hidden density identity, translation fit or arbitrary post hoc rescaling. Orthogonal whitening representations are allowed, but a general rotation changes the finite cube/cells and is not described as an exact finite-grid gauge. An exact signed-axis reflection of both outputs is tested as a positive control.

The Notebook actively reconstructs all 24 profiles and the full 35^4 distribution through five sweeps. It preserves raw preprocessing in linear/log views, all measured profile groups, sampled-source and deterministic comparisons, six two-dimensional phase-space marginal planes, prior/initial/final entropy and error diagnostics, and the V1/V2 normalization correction. Reference reconstructions are method results rather than independently measured four-dimensional truth.
