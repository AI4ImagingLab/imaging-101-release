# Implemented mathematics

The bound acquisition is the complete experimental x00y00z50 detector energy image from the authors' PMB2024 release. It is not simulated, spatially cropped, resized or corrupted when packaged. There is no independently measured spatial truth. The selected method is the published planar MURA decoding stage at the known50mm plane; 3D-MLEM and subsequent3D point-fitting are not part of this benchmark's unknown or runtime. Accorsi sparse decoding remains an actively computed scientific comparison.

For a source-plane displacement x and nominal mask-detector distance b=20mm, the shadow moves by -b*x/z. The mask magnification is m=1+b/z=1.4 at z=50mm. The detector pitch is p=14.08/256=.055mm; the reconstructed source pitch is p*z/b=.1375mm. The central repeated-mask pattern has half the full9.92mm side. Its projected side is N=round(m*9.92/p/2)=126 detector pixels, using NumPy round-to-even. The reconstruction follows the original nominal alignment; physical camera offsets and tilt are not fitted from a hidden target.

Preprocessing first casts uint32 keV samples to float32 without loss at the recorded values. Compute the1st and99th NumPy percentiles. Samples strictly below/above these bounds are replaced with the scipy.ndimage median_filter(size=3,mode='reflect') value. The selection mask is float32 and the original mask*median+(1-mask)*image arithmetic is retained. Apply gaussian_filter(sigma=1,mode='reflect',truncate=4). This robust nonlinear preprocessing precedes linear decoding; it is not an inverse regularizer or a fitted noise likelihood. No independent noise variance is assumed.

Take mask rows/columns31:93 to form the62x62 central period. If its last row or column sums to zero, roll that axis by one. Extract odd row/odd column entries into a31x31 temporary pattern. Set its central entry to zero, then replace every zero in that temporary pattern by-1. Write it back only at odd/odd positions. Other zero-filling NTHT samples stay zero. Finally roll both axes by31. This preserves the specific supplied Russo mask orientation, including the author's central-pixel convention; it is not claimed to convert arbitrary masks.

For MURA, enlarge that signed decoder from62² to126² by nearest-neighbour centre sampling: index(i)=floor((i+.5)*62/126), clipped to61. This is the exact Pillow NEAREST mapping independently captured upstream; the cleaned implementation requires only NumPy. Crop the preprocessed detector with int-truncated centred bounds. Here rows/columns65:191 produce126² samples. The local helper preserves the original fractional-crop arithmetic rather than silently rounding a different centre.

The response is r=real(ifft2(fft2(c)*fft2(d))). NumPy's backward FFT normalization makes this the circular sum r(i,j)=sum(k,l)c(k,l)d((i-k)modN,(j-l)modN), not an average or magnitude. Cast to float32 only after taking the real part. The returned signed126² response remains in summed keV units and is not calibrated Bq. There is no loss minimization, initialization, learning rate, stopping criterion or iteration count: the chosen inverse is direct. No clipping, fftshift,180degree rotation or gain normalization is applied.

Accorsi comparison uses alpha=m*.08/p, k=round(alpha)=2 and a124² zero array with the62² signed decoder at every second sample. Query the detector by scipy.interpolate.interpn linear interpolation on the author's linspace(-128-1,128+1,256) detector grid; the query extent is +/-alpha*62/2 on124 points per axis. Reshape then transpose as in the original. Circular convolution gives the signed124² comparison. This is a distinct sampling convention, so its unnormalized amplitude is not directly equated to MURA's. It is not the standard submission.

The periodic convolution forward primitive implements the discrete linear shadow model and its Euclidean adjoint with conjugated transfer FFT. Independent direct-sum, adjoint, shift-equivariance and sum-conservation cases check the FFT sign/normalization, although the real camera also has finite boundaries and penetration. Binary mask use is a nominal idealized response, not a new claim of exact energy-dependent camera calibration.

The hidden method reference is the independently run original MURA output, with all source/data hashes in validation.md. NCC uses uncentered cosine and NRMSE uses reference dynamic range. All samples are scored without alignment. The baseline self-error is zero, so a zero error threshold is inappropriate. The fixed nonzero limits NCC>=.999999 and NRMSE<=5e-6 describe float32 numerical reproduction; they are checked against repeated execution and float64 FFT sensitivity, not chosen to accept a failed scientific reconstruction. Zero/wrong/unscaled maps fail. Method agreement does not prove physical accuracy, and no unsupported forward-residual criterion is imposed on a signed decoded-response map.
