# Implemented mathematics and inference

The selected task uses the spherical branch of Maxted's eclipse model, with the explicitly corrected V2 light travel convention. It is a conditional seven-dimensional fit to all 15488 real observations. Neither the original posterior summary nor the historical initialization enters fresh inference. There is no independent stellar-property ground truth.

## Orbital and radiometric operator

The vector order is fractional radii r1,r2, inclination in degrees, fs=sqrt(e)sin(omega), fc=sqrt(e)cos(omega), mean-disk J brightness ratio S, and velocity scale K in km/s. The period is 14.2769001 days, epoch 2456023.26988 HJD, and companion/primary mass ratio .1968. The physical support requires positive radii and K, nonnegative S, 0<i<=90 degrees, e=fs²+fc²<1, and r1+r2<1-e. These restrictions are the only parameter prior in sampling; historical eccentricity/argument uncertainties are not Gaussian priors.

`periastron(t0,e,omega,inc)` minimizes the projected conjunction separation near pi/2-omega using the extracted scalar Brent update, bracket +/-pi/4, fractional tolerance 1e-8 plus 1e-10 absolute term, at most 100 iterations. Convert the minimizing true anomaly to eccentric anomaly and mean anomaly to determine periastron epoch. `eccentric_anomaly(m,e)` starts at fmod(m,2pi)+e sin(m)+e²sin(2m)/2 and applies Newton increments (m-E+e sin(E))/(1-e cos(E)) until the largest increment is<=1e-9 radians, at most 100 iterations, then wraps to[0,2pi). The observed fixtures converge in fewer iterations and match the original 9999-limit helper to float64 precision. Returned true anomaly uses the tangent half-angle formula without arbitrary branch-dependent offsets.

The first four photometric groups omit light travel time, preserving the selected approximation. Infrared and RV groups use a1=.019771142*K*P*sqrt(1-e²)/sin(i), a=(1+1/q)*a1 in solar radii, ell=a*695700000/299792458/86400 in days. The conjunction correction is ell*sin(i)*(q-1)/(q+1)*(1-e²)/(1+e sin(omega)). Initial orbital states determine barycentric line-of-sight positions; add ell*w to each time once and recompute each star's state. No extra mass fraction multiplies ell: w already includes it. Project the two separately retarded states to sky coordinates and subtract them. RV is orbit-only with historical eta=.019765685498705825. Its semiamplitude is (.019771142/eta)*K =1.0002760592995639*K. This tiny deviation from a conversion recomputed with modern constants is retained and tested.

Each spherical disk has U(mu)=1-u(1-mu)-v(1-mu)². Known white coefficients(.3532,.2606) apply to WASP and NITES; I coefficients(.2552,.2598) apply to OED and BYU. These four groups have dark secondaries. KPNO has uniform primary and secondary coefficients(.1076,.2857). The input S is a mean surface brightness ratio. The normalized component flux ratio is S*(r2/r1)². No spots, irradiation, third light, or extra shape or velocity effects are included.

`disk_grid()` constructs the published 44-node order 8 disk quadrature. Eight outer Gauss–Legendre y nodes use inner x orders 8,7,5,2 according to increasing |y|, scaled to the local circle chord. Divide weighted brightness by weighted area. A fully contained foreground disk uses this grid in its own disk, translated by the actual two-dimensional projected separation without rotating the finite grid. Fully covered or disjoint disks have exact 0/1 blocked fractions. For partial overlaps, exact circle geometry determines overlap area and common chord. Integrate the occulted region if it is below half the background area; otherwise integrate the visible complement. Region integration uses two caps or two strips plus a cap as specified in README. Outer order is clipped rounded 4*regionwidth/backgroundradius, between 4 and 8. Inner order is clipped rounded .5*outerorder*verticalheight/outerhalfrange, between 4 and 8. Positive half-integers round upward. The brightness/area integral ratio multiplies the exact region area, then divides by the full disk flux. Thus uniform-brightness occultations have exact geometric area even with sparse quadrature. This is an explicitly approximate limb-darkening operator; independent adaptive integration quantifies its discrepancy rather than claiming continuum identity.

WASP direct rows have |((1.5+((t-T0)/P mod1))mod1)-.5|<.02; all other WASP model magnitudes are zero and remain in the objective. NITES and KPNO response flags select direct rows; interpolate their relative flux linearly in HJD, then apply -2.5log10. The schedule is numerical interpolation, not finite exposure integration. OED/BYU/RV directly evaluate all rows. No observed row is discarded.

## Likelihood and numerical precision

The public ASCII decimals are stored as float64. The selected likelihood explicitly casts measurement/error columns to float32, as did the original structured reader; times, forward models, and parameters remain float64. WASP errors multiply float32(.84). RV errors become sqrt(s²+float32(.02²)). Inverse squared errors are computed in float32. These precise conventions are independently compared against frozen upstream columns. This is documented preprocessing, not a reduction of public data precision.

For each of six groups let d=y-model and w=1/sigma². The profiled offset is z=sum(w*d)/sum(w), and the component chi-square is sum(w*(d-z)²). The log likelihood is -0.5 times the six-component sum, with no division by observation count. An error vector for initialization is (d-z)*sqrt(w); squaring its norm is the same objective. There are no regularizers or extra prior penalties within physical support.

## Raw-only initialization

The least-squares residual vector uses the float32 square root of the original float32 inverse variance. Its squared norm differs from the directly summed scalar likelihood by about 0.000065–0.000080 across the sixteen original fixture vectors because of square-root rounding. Initializer diagnostic fields named joint_start_chi_squared report that residual-vector norm. The sampler, reported physical fit quality, and Judge all use the direct inverse-variance scalar sum defined above, without this additional square-root rounding. This numerical distinction is retained explicitly rather than mixing the two reports.

First fit the28RV observations with generic eccentricity/argument/velocity starts. The12trials combine e=.05,.4,.7 and omega=-2.5,-.8,.8,2.5 rad; K starts at half the observed velocity range. General SciPy trust-region least squares has bounds e[0,.9], omega[-2pi,2pi], K[.1,200], scales[.3,2,20], and max 160 function evaluations. This estimates a starting orbital shape without reading historical p0, uncertainty, chain, or posterior parameters.

The NITES and KPNO90th-minus10th percentile magnitude depths estimate a radius ratio and mean brightness start. Six joint trials combine primary radii .035,.07,.14 with impact parameters .2,.8. ## Full selected sampling budget

Draw50 walkers from the raw-fitted Gaussian curvature and reject initial points outside physical support. For1000 updates, randomly split walkers into two complementary halves. For each active walker choose a random partner in the held-fixed complementary half. With a=2, draw z=(1+U)²/2, U uniform[0,1), and propose partner+z*(active-partner). Accept with probability min(1,z^6*exp(logp(proposal)-logp(active))). Update one half, then the other. The exponent6is dimension7minus1. This is the explicit affine-invariant stretch move, not a domain sampler import.

Discard the first 500 updates. The returned point is the coordinatewise median of the remaining 25000 seven-dimensional states. It is neither a maximum likelihood nor a maximum posterior estimate. Report acceptance, finite ranges, traces, block summaries, and the limited chain length; source and fresh chains may differ statistically. Tests check moments on an independent Gaussian target and do not require seeded chain identity. The full source and fresh budgets remain50 × 1000.

## Evaluation

A separately versioned robustness extension covers extreme eccentricities in the declared physical domain. The original Newton update can diverge at e near one; simply raising its iteration count to the original 9999 does not reliably resolve those cases. After the normal 100-iteration path, only nonfinite solutions or wrapped Kepler equation residuals above 1e-10 trigger a 72-step monotone bisection on [0,2pi]. The derivative 1-e*cos(E) is positive for e<1, so this bracket contains the unique root. Input anomalies and eccentricity are validated first. No domain restriction or scientific quality boundary is narrowed. Original fixtures and complete actual posterior states are separately audited to establish that their normal values do not invoke or change under this extension. Endpoint and periodicity checks supplement the independent high-e residual tests.
