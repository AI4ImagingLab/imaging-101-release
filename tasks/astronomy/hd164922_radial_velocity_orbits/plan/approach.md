# Implemented numerical approach

## Bound observations and unknowns

Use every one of the 401 rows, preserving full BJD float64 times, m/s velocities and instrumental standard deviations. Instrument indices 0/1/2 correspond to native k/j/a with 52/276/73 rows. No nightly averaging is performed. The unknown internal vector is ordered (P,T,h,q,a) for the long-period orbit, then the same five entries for the short-period orbit, then three offsets and three positive jitter values. Here P is days, T full inferior-conjunction BJD, h and q dimensionless square-root eccentricity coordinates and a natural log amplitude with amplitude measured numerically in m/s. The output converts each orbital row to (P,T,e,omega,K), retaining the other six unknowns in separate arrays. There is no actual orbital ground truth; the frozen original method supplies a numerical reference.

## Forward model and discretisation

The model is the exact two-body line-of-sight velocity evaluated independently at each measured time; there is no spatial grid or time interpolation. For each orbit e=h^2+q^2, omega=atan2(q,h), K=exp(a). Inferior conjunction has true anomaly f=pi/2-omega. Its eccentric anomaly is Ec=2 atan[tan(f/2) sqrt((1-e)/(1+e))]. Periastron time tp=T-P(Ec-e sin Ec)/(2pi). At each time t, mean anomaly M=2pi frac((t-tp)/P). Solve E-e sin E=M. True anomaly is nu=2 atan[sqrt((1+e)/(1-e)) tan(E/2)]. The velocity is K[cos(nu+omega)+e cos omega], positive for redshift along a line of sight pointing away from the observer. Sum both planets, then add the corresponding instrument offset gamma. The acceleration and curvature are exactly zero.

The circular case evaluates its sinusoid directly. The native defensive clamping of negative P and e outside [0,.99] is present in planet_velocity, but the prior rejects these values before the forward call during fitting. No nonphysical forward extension is used for accepted estimates. The local Kepler implementation follows the original Python fallback with the same .85 initial offset and 1e-12 radian equation-residual tolerance. It adds a 100-update guard, inactive in all recorded fits. Initial E=M+.85 sign(sin M)e. For residual f=E-e sin E-M and derivatives fp=1-e cos E, fpp=e sin E, fppp=e cos E, compute d1=-f/fp, d2=-f/(fp+d1 fpp/2), d3=-f/(fp+d2 fpp/2+d2^2 fppp/6), then update E by d3 on unconverged entries. This is the explicit third-order Kepler update, not a domain solver.

## Objective and prior normalisation

For measurement n in instrument s, variance Vn=sigma_n^2+j_s^2, residual rn=y_n-vn. The negative log likelihood is one half of the sum of rn^2/Vn+log(2pi Vn). Instrument groups are summed in native k/j/a order. There is no averaging by sample count, robust clipping, free multiplicative error factor or correlated Gaussian process.

The log prior sums normalised Jeffreys period densities -log(P)-log(log(upper/lower)); Gaussian epoch densities -.5((T-mu)/sd)^2-.5 log(2pi sd^2); uniform h/q/logK bounds with -log(width); the eccentricity constraint contributes -2 log(.99); and each offset/jitter uniform prior contributes its own -log(width). Long and short period intervals are respectively [500,5000] and [1,500] days, inclusive. Epoch Gaussian centres and standard deviations are (2456779,300) and (2456277.6,25) days. h and q are strictly between -1 and 1; logK strictly between log(.001) and log(30); eccentricity inclusively between 0 and .99; offsets strictly between -10 and 10 m/s and jitters strictly between 0 and 10 m/s. The specified square-root basis has no additional Jacobian factor. Outside support the objective is positive infinity. Minimise negative log likelihood minus log prior. All normalisation constants are retained for independent stage parity.

## Initialisation, precision and optimisation

The native internal initial vector is [1206.3,2456779,sqrt(.01)cos(pi/2),.1,log(10),75.771,2456277.6,sqrt(.01)cos(pi/2),.1,0,0,1,0,2.6,2.6,2.6]. The native helper reconstruct_native preserves the source's Powell options maxiter=200, maxfev=100000, xtol=1e-8, default ftol=.0001 and unit initial directions. As in the source, it returns the mutable vector left at the last objective call. This finite baseline is retained only as an explicitly identified comparison and as one computed starting point. Independent continuation showed that its default tolerance leaves the objective under-converged; it is not the scoring reference.

The implemented primary estimate minimises the same unchanged physical log posterior more accurately. Let c be INITIAL, S=[10,100,.1,.1,.5,1,10,.1,.1,.5,1,1,1,1,1,1] in the corresponding original-coordinate units, and z=(v-c)/S elementwise. precision_fit evaluates the local physical objective at v=c+S*z. The objective remains the density in the original physical-coordinate basis: no Jacobian is added. Reinterpreting it as a density in z would add only the constant affine determinant, which cannot change the optimum. Affine conditioning prevents full BJD epochs from dominating the optimiser's relative coordinate tolerances. Powell uses maxiter=2000, maxfev=500000, xtol=1e-11 and ftol=1e-13, retaining the general optimiser's standard direction-set and line-search machinery. Return the explicit optimum result.x transformed back to physical coordinates. The physical model, prior, measurements and variance normalisation are unchanged.

The standard entry point evaluates four starts: native INITIAL; a newly computed native default-stop fit; and two prior-based alternatives obtained from INITIAL by multiplying the long period by 1 plus sign*.005 and the short period by 1 minus sign*.005, setting long h=sign*.2 and q=.1, and short h=-sign*.15 and q=.1, for sign=-1 and +1. All other starting values are unchanged. None uses a hidden reference. The selected point is the converged candidate with the lowest negative log posterior. Diagnostics retain every start's success/message, iteration/evaluation counts and objective, plus the selected index. Agreement among four starts is convergence evidence, not a proof that no other mode exists anywhere in the prior domain.

The exact same precision experiment first ran through the unchanged original Posterior.neglogprob_array API with a general-purpose Powell optimiser. All four original starts converged to objectives agreeing within1.16e-11, with maximum internal-coordinate spread3.81e-5. These outputs and the unchanged physical acceptance thresholds were frozen before the cleaned precision update. The complete early-stop source, results and earlier cleaned package are preserved privately, so this accuracy correction is explicit rather than claimed as unchanged default reproduction. No threshold was relaxed and no exact-start prescription is required in the public README.

An explicit initial vector defaults to a single local precision fit; callers may override multi_start. Optional fixed coordinates support notebook ablations. The selected full benchmark has no fixed coordinates and infers all sixteen unknowns. Noise-perturbation experiments deliberately use single warm starts to measure local sensitivity; they are not converged global-posterior MCMC. The separate source tutorial's binned-data 200-step chain is not represented as this full-data MAP experiment.

## Evaluation, comparisons and uncertainty

All ten physical orbital entries and all six instrument nuisance quantities are scored. Periods, e and K use maximum absolute error. T uses a difference modulo the reference period, and omega uses the principal circular difference. Offsets and jitters use maximum absolute m/s error. No amplitude normalisation, arbitrary permutation or fitted offset alignment is applied. Thresholds follow the independently frozen 1e-3 parameter tolerance, with 1e-4 for eccentricity and 1e-3 rad for omega. Weighted residual RMS is sqrt(mean(rn^2/Vn)) and must be <=1.05. Baseline reference is the independently converged original-API MAP result, not the native premature stopping point; tight numerical agreement does not establish physical orbit accuracy.

The active notebook recomputes the full MAP, compares its physical quantities and predicted data with the original result, retains the native early-stop comparison, fits explicit ablations, and computes noise-perturbation refits. Perturbation scatter measures local noise sensitivity, not a converged posterior or coverage guarantee. It also examines the Kepler residual and observation residuals by time and phase. All figures are derived from active arrays. The source file's original full-data configuration is preserved; the notebook's independent comparisons are explicitly distinguished from the original bound run.
